S-Corp Reasonable Compensation: How Low Can You Go?

By AE Tax Advisors Team | September 2026

The biggest S-Corp advantage is splitting income between salary and distributions. But how low can you set your salary?

IRS Factors

  • Training and experience
  • Duties and responsibilities
  • Time and effort devoted
  • Comparable salaries in your industry
  • Dividend history and company profitability

Watson v. Commissioner (2012)

CPA paid $24,000 salary on $200K+ distributions. Court reclassified distributions as wages.

Radtke v. United States (1990)

Zero salary, all dividends. Court held all payments were wages subject to FICA.

Zero salary is never acceptable. Artificially low salary invites reclassification.

Industry Benchmarks

  • Service businesses: 35% to 50% of net income
  • Product businesses: 25% to 40%
  • Real estate/investment: lower percentages may be justified

How to Protect Yourself

  • Conduct a formal compensation analysis using BLS data
  • Document your role and hours
  • Keep a written memo on reasoning
  • Adjust annually
  • Use a payroll provider

The goal is not to push salary as low as possible. It is to find the optimal balance that maximizes tax savings while staying within IRS guidelines.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if IRS says my salary is too low?

They reclassify distributions as wages, triggering back FICA taxes plus penalties.

Can I pay zero salary?

No. If you work in the business, zero salary is never acceptable.

How do I document reasonable comp?

Use BLS data, salary surveys, and comparable positions in your industry.
